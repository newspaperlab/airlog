Archer and Formula 1 explore electric air taxi links to races

The multiyear partnership covers selected events, with passenger routes and start dates still undisclosed.

Newspaper Lab · Published 2026-11-14
Source announced 2026-11-12

Archer Aviation and Formula 1 announced a multiyear partnership on November 12. Archer will attend selected Grands Prix in markets where it plans to operate across the US, Middle East, Asia and Europe. The partners will explore electric air taxi connections to circuits. No tickets, passenger routes or operating start date were announced.

Archer develops electric vertical takeoff and landing aircraft. Its Midnight is designed for repeated short urban journeys. Vertical operation can avoid the long runway used by conventional airliners, while passenger transport still depends on aircraft approval, operator approval and usable landing sites.

Archer says Midnight was displayed at the US Grand Prix in Austin and is scheduled for display in Las Vegas and Abu Dhabi later this year. An exhibition brings the aircraft to viewers. Transporting ticket holders would involve different arrangements. Race admission should not be assumed to include a flight.

Crowds converge on circuits in a short period. In our view, the location and throughput of both landing sites may matter more than airborne time. A short flight can still require a drive to the departure site and a walk to the right gate. Passenger capacity per interval, charging and waiting areas are relevant to a comparison with ground transport.

Electric propulsion avoids burning fuel onboard, while electricity generation, batteries and surface travel affect the broader footprint. Noise exposure can vary with flight paths, frequency and terrain. The announcement gives no commercial route measurements, capacity or fares. Those comparisons need a defined service and ground network.

Outbound and return demand should be evaluated separately. Fans may arrive over several hours but leave together after the race. With the same aircraft count, a departure surge can lengthen queues. This is an editorial inference, not an event forecast or contractual promise. Event-specific peak demand and waiting space would need to be considered.

The nearer event for fans is the planned exhibition at two races. Passenger service remains subject to approval, operating arrangements and announcements of sales and locations. Compare future airborne time with venue access, alternatives during weather disruption and the last departure after a race.
