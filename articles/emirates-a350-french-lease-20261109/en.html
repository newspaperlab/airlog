Emirates completes a 12-year financing for two A350s

A French Lease and Shariah-compliant structure support aircraft acquisition; costs remain undisclosed.

Newspaper Lab · Published 2026-11-12
Source announced 2026-11-09

Emirates, Dubai Islamic Bank and Natixis CIB announced on November 9 the completion of a 12-year financing supporting two A350-900 acquisitions in 2026. It combines a French Lease framework with Shariah-compliant financing in one transaction. This is funding news, not a new-route launch. The release does not establish each aircraft’s delivery date or first assignment.

Emirates is a Dubai-based international airline with a long-haul focus. The A350-900 is an Airbus twin-engine widebody. Acquiring aircraft requires both selecting equipment and arranging how to pay over time. This transaction covers two aircraft, not automatically the airline’s entire A350 order or all future deliveries.

DIB and Natixis CIB provided financing and arranged the deal. DIB is a Dubai-based Islamic bank; Natixis CIB is the corporate and investment banking business within France’s Groupe BPCE. The concrete development is their combination of financing frameworks for one acquisition transaction. Emirates describes diversification of funding, but does not quantify savings against earlier deals.

A lease structures rights to use an aircraft and associated financial obligations. “French Lease” describes a legal and financial framework, not an obligation to fly French routes. Shariah compliance concerns structuring under Islamic finance principles. The release does not publish cash flows, detailed ownership stages or final purchase terms, so general explanations cannot be treated as this contract’s clauses.

The 12-year term is also different from aircraft lifespan or a guarantee of service on a particular route. Fleet allocation and schedules are separate decisions. A financing maturity does not mean the same type will serve the same airport for 12 years. Seating, flight numbers and delivery schedules need separate operational announcements.

Passengers may see no immediate service change, but financing underpins long-term fleet investment. Our interpretation is that access to different funding pools can reduce reliance on one channel. It does not establish cheaper tickets or a guaranteed destination. Demand, deployment and operating costs sit between financing and pricing decisions.

The transaction value, each institution’s share, detailed payment terms and aircraft schedules remain undisclosed. We therefore make no numerical cost comparison with other airlines. Completion of financing is the confirmed announcement; it is not the start date of aircraft operations. The story illustrates why funding milestones matter alongside headline aircraft orders.
