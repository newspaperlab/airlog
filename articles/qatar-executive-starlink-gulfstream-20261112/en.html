Qatar Executive finishes Starlink installations on its G700 and G650ER fleet

The Qatar Airways Group charter division has completed the retrofit on its two ultra-long-range Gulfstream types.

Newspaper Lab · Published 2026-11-13
Source announced 2026-11-12

On November 12 Qatar Executive reported Starlink Wi-Fi installed and available across its Gulfstream G700 and G650ER fleet. November 12 is the date it announced completion. The release gives neither the final aircraft’s installation date nor each jet’s first service date. Charter customers should identify the aircraft assigned to their trip.

Qatar Executive is the group’s private charter division. Its floating fleet can reposition aircraft to meet demand, unlike a passenger timetable that sells individual seats. The G700 and G650ER serve ultra-long-range nonstop missions. In our assessment, standardizing the equipment across these types makes the installed internet system more predictable if another aircraft of either type is assigned.

Cabin Wi-Fi involves two links: a device connects to the cabin network, while the aircraft terminal exchanges data with satellites. Starlink’s low-Earth-orbit satellites are closer than geostationary ones. Shorter paths can reduce the time a signal takes to make a round trip. In a video call, the delay before a reply is a separate concern from file download speed.

The company says its engineering staff and Qatar Airways maintenance, repair and overhaul teams performed most installations in-house. Connecting an external antenna to cabin equipment involves power, wiring, placement and operational approvals. The announcement gives no per-aircraft installation duration or component costs.

Customers now have more reason to plan messaging or meetings on long flights. The release does not offer guaranteed route-specific speeds or latency. Passenger load on the network, flight path and satellite service conditions can influence the experience. For time-critical work, check usage terms and where a connection is expected, and keep offline copies of essential material.

Satellite visibility and service permission are separate. Starlink’s aviation guidance describes coverage over international waters and lists countries that authorize in-motion use. Receiving a signal does not establish identical conditions in every airspace. When ground access or a particular country matters, ask the operator about the itinerary and expected availability.

The 170 widebodies cited in the same release belong to a separate Qatar Airways scheduled-fleet rollout. The reported completion covers the G700 and G650ER charter types. Timelines for other charter aircraft and individual aircraft performance remain undisclosed.
