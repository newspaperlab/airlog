Aviation technology / EXPLAINED

Skywise brings pilot training into its digital portfolio

The integration covers MATe, VPT and Flight eLearning. Virtual cockpit practice is intended to support preparation before practical training.

Newspaper Lab · Published 2026-11-04
Source announcement 2026-11-02

Who is making the change?

Skywise, Airbus’s digital services company, announced the training portfolio integration on November 2. Aviation relies on connected flows of information across planning, maintenance, operations and education. This development concerns training tools and the way users access them.

What the tools do

MATe supports aircraft-system learning, while VPT provides a virtual environment for practicing procedures. Flight eLearning is also included. Procedure practice is different from a flying game: the educational aim is to understand checks and action sequences in context, rather than simply control an on-screen aircraft.

Where it could help

Our analysis focuses on access to repeated preparation. Familiarity with a cockpit layout may allow limited training time to be used more effectively for questions and practice. The integration announcement alone does not establish improved learning outcomes or safety performance. Course approval, assessment and instructor oversight remain separate issues.

What has not been announced

November 2 is the announcement date, not a universal changeover date for every operator. Customers need to check access arrangements, contracts and compatibility with existing courses. For passengers, it is an example of aviation’s less visible digital infrastructure.
