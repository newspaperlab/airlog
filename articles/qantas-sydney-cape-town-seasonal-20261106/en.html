Qantas opens Sydney–Cape Town nonstop bookings for an October 2027 start

Sales opened with the November 6 announcement. Three weekly Boeing 787-9 flights are planned for the South African summer.

Newspaper Lab · Published 2026-11-10
Source announced 2026-11-06

Qantas has opened bookings for seasonal nonstop flights between Sydney and Cape Town. Sales began with the November 6 announcement, while the first Sydney departure, QF89, is scheduled for October 19, 2027. The first season runs through March 24, 2028. The booking date and the year of travel are separate.

The Australian airline already flies nonstop from Sydney and Perth to Johannesburg. Qantas says some passengers use those services to connect onward to Cape Town. The expansion gives Cape Town its own nonstop link from Australia. The established Johannesburg flights may still suit passengers whose final destination is there.

Qantas plans three flights a week on Tuesdays, Fridays and Sundays using the Boeing 787-9 Dreamliner, a long-haul twin-engine widebody. QF89 is scheduled to leave Sydney at 09:15 and arrive in Cape Town at 14:40 local time. The airline quotes around 14 hours and 25 minutes. The local clocks differ by time zone. Travelers making lodging plans should use the Cape Town arrival date.

Seasonal capacity and fares

The nearly 30,000 additional seats cited by the company describe capacity between Australia and South Africa once the service operates. They do not measure passengers carried or tickets already sold. The announced economy return fare started at AUD 2,470. Availability, date and fare terms determine the amount at checkout.

A nonstop can remove a connection. In our analysis, frequency matters when comparing the whole trip. A three-times-weekly schedule can suit flexible travelers, while a traveler with fixed dates may find a different connection more practical. A passenger starting elsewhere in Australia also needs to count the leg to Sydney. Cutting time from one flight segment changes the itinerary only in combination with the waits around it.

Qantas says its 1952 scheduled passenger service took 66 hours to reach Johannesburg via Perth, the Cocos Islands and Mauritius. The airports and route differ from the new 14-hour-25-minute schedule, so the figures are not a direct speed comparison. They illustrate how longer nonstop sectors can remove stages previously handled at intermediate airports.

Qantas credits deliveries of new 787-9s expected in late 2027 with enabling the route. The release does not establish year-round service or an increase after the first season. Match lodging and ground transportation to the seasonal calendar and the ticketed dates. Booking has started and the first-season schedule is public. Aircraft delivery and flight operations are future milestones.
